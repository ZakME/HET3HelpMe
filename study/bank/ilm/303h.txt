+ narrow pads, for traction and less wear
- wide pads, for maximum flotation
- multi-grouser low-profile pads, for grip
- rubber pads, to protect the surface
E: Narrow pads concentrate ground pressure for traction on hard surfaces and cause less twisting stress on the chain.

@303h|Obj 1|
Q: Why do wide track pads cause extra undercarriage wear on hard surfaces?
+ They increase chain twisting against the rollers
- They raise ground pressure too high on rock
- They make the sprocket run out of pitch
- They prevent the track from oscillating
E: On hard ground the wide pad levers and twists the chain, which wears the links and rollers. It also gives less ground pressure per square inch, so less traction.

@303h|Obj 1|
Q: Which combination gives an undercarriage the GREATEST load-carrying ability?
+ A long track frame and wide pads
- A short track frame and wide pads
- A long track frame and narrow pads
- A short track frame and narrow pads
E: Wider pads = more flotation = more load capacity, and a longer frame supports more load.

@303h|Obj 1|
Q: When wider pads are fitted to increase machine stability, what changes about the track frame?
+ It's mounted further out. The frame design is the same.
- A different, heavier track frame design is required
- The track frame is lengthened to spread the load
- The frame is moved forward under the blade
E: The frame design is the same for narrow or wide pads. Only the distance from the side of the machine changes, which widens the stance.

@303h|Obj 1|
Q: How does a track-type loader's track frame differ from a dozer's?
+ Loader: forward and rigid. Dozer: rearward and oscillating.
- Loader: oscillating. Dozer: rigid.
- Both rigid, but the loader frame is longer
- Loader: rearward, to counterweight the bucket
E: The loader needs forward stability while lifting and must not tip sideways (rigid). The dozer carries less load up front, and its oscillating frames give traction and ride comfort.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Which additional component does an oscillating (swing) track frame require?
+ An equalizer bar
- A bogie roller
- A carrier roller
- A recoil cylinder
E: The equalizer bar (pivot shaft or hard bar) supports the front of the machine and lets the frames move independently.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Why is the equalizer bar pin often neglected and prone to premature wear?
+ Its remote location hides leaks and plugged grease lines
- It is a sealed, lifetime-lubricated joint that can't be serviced
- It carries no load, so it's left off the PM schedule
- It is replaced every time the track is split
E: Its hard-to-reach location means lubrication failures go unnoticed. The pin and bushings then wear, and repair often requires removing the bar (more downtime).

- Closer to the centre of the machine
- At the front idler yoke
- On the equalizer bar centre pin
E: Low drive: the pivot is at the rear, in line with the final drive. High drive: the pivot is closer to the centre of the machine.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Why is a rigid track frame used on excavators, cranes and draglines instead of an oscillating frame?
+ A load swung across the machine would make it tilt
- Rigid frames give a smoother ride while travelling
- Rigid frames reduce track and roller wear
- Oscillating frames can't support the machine weight
E: Machines that swing a load across their axis need a stable base. The trade-off is a rougher ride.

+ two side links, one pin and one bushing
- one link, two pins and one bushing
- two side links and two pins
- one link, one pin, one bushing and one shoe bolt
E: Two track (side) links with a pin and a bushing, both pressed into the links.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: A master pin in a track chain is often identified by:
+ a small dimple in the end of the pin
- a plug and stopper in the pin end
- a paint mark on the link
- a larger outside diameter along the full length
E: The master pin usually has a dimple (other pins are flat on the ends), though some have one end larger than the other. A stopper and plug identifies a LUBRICATED pin.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Tracks that use a master link with a master PIN have what type of track pins?
+ Non-lubricated
- Sealed and lubricated (SALT)
- Grease-lubricated through a fitting
- Either type, depending on design
E: The guide says master pin tracks have non-lubricated pins. Split master link tracks may have either type.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Before joining a split master link, what must be done to the mating surfaces?
+ Clean off all paint and debris
- Coat them with anti-seize
- Apply thread locker to the serrations
- Install a shim pack between the halves
E: Clean, paint-free mating surfaces (coarse or fine tooth). The bolts are fine thread and must be properly torqued.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: How can you tell a sealed and lubricated track (SALT) from a non-lubricated track?
+ A stopper and plug in the end of the pins
- A dimple in the end of each pin
- Grease fittings on each track link
- Bushings that are visibly rotated
E: Lubricated pins have a stopper and plug in the pin end (where they were filled).

@303h|Obj 2|ct
Q: A SALT track starts making a loud snapping or squeaking sound at certain joints during travel. What is the most likely cause?
+ Those joints have lost their lubricant
- Track tension is too loose and slapping
- The carrier rollers are seized
- The track shoe bolts are loose
E: When lubricated joints lose their oil, the dry pin and bushing contact causes a loud snapping sound, and internal wear speeds up.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Which track shoe is typically used on a track-type loader to avoid tearing up the ground?
+ A multi-grouser pad with low grousers
- A single grouser pad with a high grouser
- An ice-lugged single grouser pad
- A flat pad with no grousers
E: Multi-grouser low-profile pads give traction without digging in as hard as a single grouser.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Which sprocket design can be replaced WITHOUT splitting the track?
+ Segmented, bolt-on sprocket
- One-piece weld-on sprocket
- One-piece bolt-on sprocket
- None. All sprockets need the track split.
E: Segments can be changed by rotating the sprocket so each segment becomes accessible. One-piece sprockets need the track split and removed.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: What is a disadvantage of a LOW (conventional) drive crawler compared to a high drive?
+ The final drive can be shock loaded by rocks
- It has more internal pin and bushing wear from extra pivot points
- It needs both front and rear idlers
- It cannot use an oscillating frame
E: On a low drive, the sprocket and final drive are in the shock path. A high drive keeps the final drive up out of harm's way.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: What is a disadvantage of the HIGH drive undercarriage?
+ More internal pin and bushing wear
- The final drive is exposed to shock loads from rocks
- The sprocket must be welded on
- It cannot be used with bogie rollers
E: The elevated sprocket adds extra direction changes (pivot points) in the chain, which increases internal pin and bushing wear. High drives also need front and rear idlers.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Why should undercarriage components be replaced together rather than mixing new and used parts?
+ Mixing them wears the new parts faster
- New parts can't be installed on a used track frame
- It is required for warranty only
- Used parts are a different pitch size
E: Mismatched wear (e.g., a new sprocket on a stretched chain) causes poor fit and speeds up wear on the new parts.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Carrier (pedestal) rollers are used to:
+ support the weight of the upper track chain
- support the weight of the machine on the track chain
- guide the track into the sprocket
- absorb recoil shock from the idler
E: Carrier rollers are on top of the track frame and hold up the chain. Track rollers (bottom) support the machine weight.

@303h|Obj 2|

+ Between the front idler and the final drive sprocket
- Across two bottom track rollers
- Across the equalizer bar and track frame
- From the rear idler to the ground
E: Double carrier rollers: straightedge across them. High drive: across the sprocket and carrier roller, or idler to sprocket if there is no carrier roller.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: Why is a SINGLE flange track roller commonly placed right after the idler (and in front of the sprocket or rear idler)?
+ To give the track room to align with the other rollers
- Single flange rollers carry more of the machine's load
- To reduce cost at the least-loaded positions
- To prevent the track from jumping the idler
E: The single flange gives the chain room to line up as it enters and leaves. Rollers are usually staggered single and double flange to keep the track centred.

@303h|Obj 2|

+ track roller mounting bolts to shear off
- carrier rollers to over-tighten the track
- equalizer bar bushings to seize
- idler grease to over-pressurize
E: A sheared roller bolt can let the roller fall off or jam in another roller or the sprocket.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: What is the advantage of bogie-style track rollers?
+ They pivot independently for better ride and traction
- They eliminate the need for a recoil spring
- They are cheaper to maintain than fixed rollers
- They allow the track to run looser without de-tracking
E: With a conventional frame, ground contact is limited to the object being crossed. Bogies conform to the terrain.

+ The O-ring both seals and loads the faces
- The faces should be greased during assembly
- The O-ring only seals. A spring loads the faces.
- Used seal faces are dull and safe to handle
E: The O-ring (toric ring) seals and, when compressed, puts pressure on the faces. The faces are precision lapped and easily damaged, and used faces can be razor sharp.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: What is the MAIN function of the track idler?
+ To let the chain change direction gradually
- To drive the track chain
- To support the upper track chain
- To set track tension and recoil
E: The idler gives a gradual change of direction. Along with the recoil mechanism and track tension, it also helps absorb shock.

@303h|Obj 2|
Q: In a typical grease cylinder and coil spring track adjuster, what does each part do?
+ The grease cylinder adjusts track tension. The spring absorbs recoil shock.
- The spring adjusts tension. The grease cylinder absorbs shock.
- Both adjust tension
- The grease cylinder absorbs shock and the spring retracts the idler
E: Pump grease in to push the idler forward (tighter). Release grease to loosen. The heavy spring is the recoil portion.

@303h|Obj 2|ct

+ The idler moves forward and the track tightens
- The idler moves rearward and the track loosens
- The recoil spring is released
- Nothing until the engine is started
E: Grease pushes the idler forward = tighter. Caution: the grease is under high pressure and the spring is under very high tension.

- Nothing. Track chains are symmetrical.
- Faster carrier roller and idler wear only

E: A reversed chain increases internal wear between the pin and bushing, as well as bushing wear where it contacts the sprocket.

+ To turn pins and bushings before the bushings wear through
- To decide when to change the final drive oil
- To set the recoil spring preload for the season
- Because wear measurements set the engine derate
E: If bushings wear through, you lose the chance to turn them and both pins and bushings become scrap.

Q: What is a likely effect of running a crawler with its tracks adjusted too TIGHT?
+ Faster pin, bushing and sprocket wear, and lost power
- The track jumping off the idler when turning
- Reduced sprocket wear from a firmer mesh
- Better fuel economy from less track slap
E: Over-tension adds load on every joint and the sprocket, which raises friction and wear and robs power. Too loose can cause de-tracking and whipping. (General knowledge + guide.)
