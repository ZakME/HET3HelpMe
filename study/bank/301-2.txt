@301h|Pressure tests|hl ct
Q: Pump inlet pressure reads below 12 psia. Which is a likely cause?
+ Restricted inlet line
- Air leaking into the inlet line
- Worn pump
- Relief valve set too low
E: The pump normally pulls a slight vacuum at its inlet. A restriction, too much pump speed or the wrong (too thick) oil makes it pull harder, so inlet pressure drops below about 12 psia and the pump cavitates. A leaking inlet line does the opposite: air gets in and inlet pressure reads higher than normal.

@301h|Pressure tests|hl ct
Q: Pump inlet pressure reads higher than normal (less vacuum than usual). This may indicate:
+ an air leak in the inlet line
- a plugged suction strainer
- excessive pump speed
- oil that is too thick
E: A pump normally pulls its inlet slightly below atmospheric pressure (a vacuum) as it draws oil up the suction line. A leak in that line (loose clamp, cracked hose, bad fitting) lets the pump suck in air instead, which fills in the vacuum, so the gauge reads higher (closer to atmospheric) and the oil gets aerated. A plugged strainer, thick oil or too much speed do the opposite: they make it harder to pull oil, so inlet pressure drops (more vacuum) and the pump cavitates.
