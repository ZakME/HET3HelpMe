+ It stays engaged or disengaged until the operator moves it
- It engages automatically when engine speed rises
- It is spring applied and releases when the pedal is pushed
- It disengages automatically under overload
E: Push the lever to engage and pull it to disengage. It stays put until moved, because the linkage locks over centre.

Q: An overcentre clutch on a pump drive slips and smells hot under load. The lever goes over centre with much less force than spec. What is the most likely cause and fix?
+ Worn discs. Adjust the ring to spec pull force.
- The pilot bearing is worn. Replace the pilot bearing.
- The remote linkage is too short. Lengthen it.
- The release bearing is dry. Grease it.
E: Low engagement force = not enough clamp load. Wear increases clearance, and adjusting the ring restores the clamp force. If it's already at the adjustment limit, the discs and plates need replacing.

@303k|Obj 2|
Q: When troubleshooting a slipping electromagnetic clutch, where should you start?
+ Battery voltage, wiring and ground
- Replace the clutch coil and hub
- Check the clutch friction material
- Check the compressor oil level
E: Low voltage, bad wiring or a poor ground means a weak magnet, low clamp force and slip, which can lead to a catastrophic failure. The air gap must also be within spec.

Q: A compressor electromagnetic clutch slips. There are 12.6 V at the battery but only 10.1 V at the clutch coil connector with the clutch engaged. What is the next step?
+ Voltage drop test the feed and ground circuits
- Replace the clutch coil, since it's drawing too much
- Reduce the air gap to below spec to compensate
- Charge the battery and retest
E: A 2.5 V loss under load means resistance in the circuit (a connection, wire, relay or ground). The coil can't build full magnetic clamp force.
